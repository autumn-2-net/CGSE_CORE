The optional vector-allocation admission copied and reduced ordinary mixed-sign

It never admitted a vector arm on this case. Those unsuccessful scans still

CountModelViews now records whether every row has one sign among its unfixed
coefficients and whether at least four possible capacity-group rows remain,
using the existing charged shape scan. CountVectorPartition
requires this property: capacity-group rows are positive and uniform, while
every remaining bin row must have coefficients of one sign. A row mixing signs
therefore rules out this particular decomposition without copying the matrix.
Fewer than four positive equal-coefficient groups, each with two to eight free
terms, also rules it out. These groups must ultimately be disjoint, so fewer
than eight unfixed coordinates cannot qualify. The metadata only rejects
impossible shapes; overlap, capacities and bin consistency still require the
specialist's ordinary checks.
The portfolio skips only that inapplicable admission; its ordinary search,
scalar partition attempts, shared cap and verification stay intact.

This is a domain-scoped scheduling hint, never an infeasibility certificate.
Zero coefficients do not contribute a sign. Fixed terms belong in the RHS,
including nonzero and large signed offsets. Each compiled view computes its own
hint in its own coordinates. If shared root bounds change the domain before
the view's first admission, the old hint cannot refuse it: fixing a coordinate
may expose an allocation that was absent in the original domains. Manually supplied cost estimates have
no negative structural evidence and retain the conservative admission path.
The existing one-attempt-per-view policy is unchanged; a later bound-only
change does not itself schedule a second allocation admission.
