CGSE continuation audit (2026-10-09)
==================================

There are two different boundaries. A caller yielding between step() calls
already retains the object's fields. Exhausting an algorithm's own local quota
used to finish several of those objects as UNKNOWN, after which their caller
closed them. Keeping the outer planner alive did not keep these inner searches.

Frontiers retained across local quotas
-------------------------------------
The existing LCG, PB/CDCL, JUMP, weighted LP/LCG, cardinality and bin-packing
frontiers retain their queues, clauses, bases, walk state or branch stacks.
IntegerCountSearch, CountScale, CountViewSearch, CountSchedule and the stock-view
portfolio already preserve owned work between scheduler turns.

This change adds opt-in local-quota continuation for:

* CountMeetInMiddle: projected coordinates, prefix enumerators, the left table,
  right-table cursor and original-row verification cursor.
* CountSeparator: incremental preparation, completed bucket tables, current
  tuple and reconstruction state. Its CountInterfaceSearch child also retains
  interface memo entries and active/paused conditional solvers at a parent cap.
* CountDecisionDiagram: current and next frontiers, parent/value cursor, prefix
  witnesses and proof layers. Preparation now occurs in step(), so a short
  constructor quota cannot destroy the object before retention is selected.
* CountDomainSearch: the encoded PB frontier or the wide-domain LCG frontier.
  In particular, the learning-rate root worker no longer closes a paused child.
* CountBoolean: the unit-weight search's trail, propagation queue, explanations
  and learned conflicts.

The main branch transfers unfinished specialists to CountViewSearch under the
same rows, bounds and reduced coordinates. It still visits the next existing
strategy first. The usual policy later resumes an owned specialist; no second
table or encoding is constructed. An adopted specialist cannot suppress the
initial visits of the proving engines. Duplicate ownership is refused. Closing
or replacing the branch's views closes the transferred objects before their
coordinate inverse is discarded.

These adopted arms export revalidated positive candidates only. Their local
infeasibility, trial restriction or interrupted proof is not reinterpreted as a
global impossibility. Direct exhaustive results still retain the existing
original-scope proof and verification rules.

Accounting and fairness
-----------------------
Continuation does not reset or refund the request's work budget. Local deadlines
use saturating arithmetic, reject nonpositive quanta and check cancellation and
an exhausted shared budget before restarting. Work done by interface children
also belongs to the parent's local allowance. Paused polling does not perform
new search or allocate another workspace.

Pause only at coherent step boundaries. The hard request budget and cancellation
remain checked inside arithmetic and enumeration. Memory refusal, precision
failure, unsupported structure and exhausted finite domains are terminal for
that attempt; they are different from a resumable time slice.

Decision-diagram growth required an additional guard: its retained node payload
may use at most one eighth of the bytes available when it is constructed. The
initial admitted workspace remains separately charged. This optional search
cannot grow to occupy all remaining request memory. Scheduler progress counts
completed layers, not raw node creation; exponential frontier growth is cost,
not evidence that a witness is close. Reservations are logical estimates, not
measurements of JVM heap or RSS.

Bounded attempts that deliberately remain terminal
--------------------------------------------------
Not every local limit is a lost scheduler continuation. CountQuickSolve and its
component/conditioning/source-face users make bounded attempts before trying
other representations or restrictions. CountNetwork, CountPartition, CountPacking
and CountComponents still finish their particular local attempt at their cap.
CountDiving, CountLatticeRepair, CountFeasibilityPump, CountNeighborhood and the
soft/core-guided probes retain fields between step() calls, but their configured
attempt/face/core limits still end that attempt.

CountAffineLattice, CountKernelSearch and CountDiophantine remain bounded numeric
candidate arms. The conditional wrapper keeps them across its own handoffs, but
does not extend their numeric hard cap indefinitely. Several arithmetic routines
can currently stop while changing a factorization or building a projected model;
catching that stop as a resumable pause would be unsound. A future extension must
first introduce explicit arithmetic checkpoints, preserve the original scope,
and justify extra portfolio turns with an equal-budget counterexample. This
change does not pretend those numeric arms now have general continuation support.

Regression and measurement
--------------------------
CountContinuationTest compares 960 engine/order cases with exhaustive finite
boxes, including signed and >64-bit coordinates. Interrupted and uninterrupted
searches must agree, and proof journals are replayed independently. Additional
cases cover five planted Boolean instances beyond the old quota (three orders
each), the lazy integer wrapper, nested interfaces, ownership/scope rejection,
cancelled/exhausted/overflowing resumes, memory limits and four-worker budgets
with and without expansion.

tests/benchmarks/compare_conditional.py --campaign continuations runs the same
256-case harness against two revisions. It is a specialist-only comparison:
old one-shot arms leave the remaining request budget unused, whereas retained
arms receive further small turns within that same request cap. Its witness
count is not a measurement of whole-planner speed. compare_planning.py separately
checks complete plans and costs under both search and aggregate work caps, with
cold/warm/replaced catalogs. Both scripts record source/harness/input identities.

Captured inventory plus full-recipe-library replay and expanded shuffled graph
fixtures exercise the production entry points. Those are offline replays, not
live Minecraft or GT-machine validation.
