Follow-up F2 gap: incompatible repeated promotions
-------------------------------------------------
With several destructive rules on one physical tool, moving only the newly
blocked variant to the front can undo a previously useful order. A constructive
family starts at tool:n+levels+1: light uses subtract one, while heavy rule k
requires at least k+1 and returns tool:k. Light n times, then heavy levels..1,
then ordinary finishing is executable. On 8ef89118, 76 of 96 source/slot-order
variants solve; 20 remain UNKNOWN despite that literal witness.

Recording every local failure as a permanent order preference was insufficient:
damage accumulated earlier can make a later harmless use appear responsible,
creating contradictory hints. The retained approach instead compares one-step
effects on the proposal's selected activation keys after repeated plain failures.
It builds a bounded preference graph over selected variants and tries one stable
topological rank. Cycles do not establish a deadlock; they leave the plain order.
Ordinary dependencies can override a rank, and exact restoration remains required.

The analysis is capped at 64 variants and min(4096, one quarter of the order
continuation's remaining work), with a 256-work minimum. Simple three-rule cases
get a third ordinary attempt before analysis; longer interactions start after
two failures. Only one structural word can be added. Its rejection restores the
interrupted plain candidate, retaining all eight ordinary attempts inside the
same original 32768/one-sixteenth lifetime allowance. This is a scheduling hint,
not a learned precedence, an expanded state domain or a global negative proof.

Follow-up F2 gap: edge preferences versus a whole matching
--------------------------------------------------------
A legal one-firing flow need not preserve the tools needed later. Consider
u accepting b/c, v accepting a/b, and a later read requiring a. The breadth-first
matcher can assign b to u, then a to v because a is free. The alternating path
which moves u to c and gives b to v is longer, but avoids losing a. Sorting the
edges does not make ordinary breadth-first augmentation minimize total penalty.

An initial 48-case consumed-return family already solved on 267de751 by changing
activation/order choices. Its original positive controls remain archived. In a
second family, u returns its tool unchanged and b has higher declared guaranteed
capacity than c, fixing that preference across inventory orders. Warm a:1,b:n,c:1,
then use c in u and b in v for each pair, then read a for every part: this is a
literal witness. With n=1/2/7/31 and 12 source/slot/stock orders, the old public
entry solves 24/48; the other 24 are UNKNOWN despite the physical witness.

Existing qualitative/quantitative recovery now optionally improves the complete
matching before consuming its tools. It reuses the negative-cycle cancellation
approach already present in CountNetwork on the feasible entity flow itself,
with unused supply represented by a reservoir.
Every atomic exchange preserves all demand totals, eligibility and supply counts.
An exact integer objective ranks total qualitative penalty before total marginal
wear, including the previous quantitative feedback when present. It is a bounded
positive candidate improvement, not a new count cut or temporal infeasibility proof.
