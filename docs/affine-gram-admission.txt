Affine admission and exact integer Gram factors, 2026-10-10
=========================================================

Problem and scope
-----------------
The canonical generated/wide-equality-n64-w1000000 case is known SAT, but
168b824b exhausts the 20M main-count budget. Reduction leaves 53 coordinates
and 16 independent equations, hence a 37-dimensional integer kernel. The old
48-coordinate entrance limit prevents the affine strategy from trying it.

CountAffineLattice now admits ambient dimension up to 128 only when the larger
model has at least two weighted exact equations and its estimated free dimension
is at most 48. The estimate subtracts the equation count from the unfixed variable
count; it is not a rank proof. Exact intersections must actually leave at most
48 basis vectors before the final LLL/nearest-plane search. Dependent equations
cannot bypass that second check. Intermediate basis stabilization still uses
the reserved ambient workspace. CountKernelSearch accepts the same bounded
ambient space with a kernel of at most 48 dimensions.

This remains an optional candidate strategy. Failed admission, precision limits,
unproductive trial faces and interrupted searches do not imply infeasibility.
Original-coordinate bounds/rows and executable primitive prefixes still validate
accepted count-stage results. Request budgets, full-model admission and fallback
semantics are unchanged.

Exact factorization and retained work
------------------------------------
CountGramSchmidt computes integer dot products G_ij = b_i . b_j, then the exact
unit-lower-triangular factors G = L D L^T:

  r_ij = G_ij - sum(k < j, L_ik * L_jk * D_k)
  L_ij = r_ij / D_j                  (j < i)
  D_i  = r_ii

The implicit diagonal of L is one. There is no floating-point conversion.
Integer dot products avoid repeatedly updating rational orthogonal vectors in
the ambient space. Existing exact LLL updates and nearest-plane reconstruction
consume the same coefficients and norms. The 512-bit basis and 2048-bit rational
precision guards remain; a guard failure terminates only this candidate attempt.

Each completed projection or norm is retained. A local quantum can suspend the
search between those checkpoints without restarting a row. Each dot-product
coordinate and lower-triangular recurrence term is charged; cancellation and
the hard request cap remain active inside those loops. This changes actual
arithmetic work, not a strategy work multiplier.

Before basis/factor allocation, the optional reservation is
8192 + 512 * ambient * free + 1536 * free^2 + 256 * rows bytes. When all variables
are free, this equals the former 2048 * ambient^2 workspace term. Fixed variables
no longer imply allocating a full square basis. This is a bounded workspace
estimate, not a measurement of JVM heap or process RSS.

Reference: fplll's MatGSO describes exact integer Gram matrices and retained
Gram-Schmidt coefficients. The recurrence here is independently implemented;
no fplll code or native dependency is included.

  https://fplll.github.io/fplll/classMatGSO.html (consulted 2026-10-10)

Equal-budget result
-------------------
Against 168b824b, the complete 274-model corpus in three deterministic orders
uses the same 20M search/compilation account caps, 256 MiB reservation limit,
one worker and no wall deadline. These are main integer-count comparisons,
not complete GraphPlanner runs.

                   Before (PASS / UNKNOWN / unsupported)    After
  Original order                 252 / 16 / 6           253 / 15 / 6
  Shuffled order 1               250 / 18 / 6           252 / 16 / 6
  Shuffled order 2               252 / 16 / 6           253 / 15 / 6

There are four gained conclusions, no lost answers and no contradictions:
  wide-equality-n64-w1000000: original and shuffled order 2;
  sparse-equality-n48-s1: shuffled order 1;
  wide-equality-n64-w10000: shuffled order 1.

The first case now takes 6,680,921 and 6,931,399 recorded work in the two solved
orders, versus approximately 19.99M and UNKNOWN. Its shuffled order 1 remains
UNKNOWN at the same cap. The focused 45-model comparison uses different shuffles
because the deterministic stream depends on the selected corpus; it solves all
three orders of this case and must not be substituted for the complete corpus.

Total recorded work across all 822 runs decreases from 1,505,615,620 to
1,470,984,329. Some already-solved cases cost more: the largest increase is
1,245,560 for wide-equality-n64-w31/order=2. The separate 72 parity/neighbor
conclusions are unchanged; their work increases from 40,446,078 to 40,479,391.
These results do not imply uniformly cheaper search or eliminate order sensitivity.

Validation
----------
CountGramSchmidtTest compares the factors and norms against an independent
explicit-vector Gram-Schmidt implementation on 261 matrices, including signed
coefficients, 49/64/96/128 ambient dimensions and a near-dependent 400-bit case.
Across the 260 ordinary matrices, arithmetic-loop charges decrease from 545,397
to 325,433. This is not a wall-time or whole-solver speed measurement.

The test also checks expanded affine and projected-kernel witnesses against
original rows, large signed offsets, identical sliced/whole result and work,
dependent rank estimates, empty/dependent bases, memory refusal, cancellation,
hard cutoffs and sequential handoff between worker threads.

63 standard programs passed. The maintained positive count boundary now pins
the newly solved original model; all 17 boundary records pass. Graph regressions
validate 239 curated and 800 independently planted cases in three recipe orders
(3,117 checks). Captured inventory plus 5,903 manual and 62,284 exported recipes
passes all 19 maintained requests in each of serial, four-worker base-budget and
four-worker expanded-budget mode (57 verified requests). This replays existing
network captures; it is not a new live AE/server test.

Offline assemble passed. All 200 production Java files match the source JAR
byte-for-byte. Input, source, harness and report fingerprints are retained in
tests/provenance/affine-gram-admission-20261010.json. Generated inputs, temporary
experiments and raw reports remain under ignored build directories.

Remaining boundary
------------------
15 original-order models remain UNKNOWN: eight independently known SAT and
seven independently known UNSAT. The six unbounded finite-conversion exclusions
are unchanged. Larger sparse equalities, a wide scalar equality/subset case,
objective-bound proofs and unfavorable orders remain numeric targets. The
128-coordinate / 48-kernel admission is still bounded, not a general lattice
solver. Large graph closures, complete end-to-end attribution (J) and host-wide
residency accounting (H) remain separate audit gaps.
