Bounded integer LP learning and current-inventory derivation templates
====================================================================

CountLcg.integerRelaxation() is separate from learnedRelaxation(). The latter
retains its Boolean projection, rounding and propagation ordering. Integer
LP proposals use x = lower + z and the actual upper-lower width, including
fixed columns. Exact cut activity uses coefficient * endpoint. Numerical
rounding is performed on z before adding the BigInteger lower endpoint, so
large offsets do not lose units through a double conversion.

Numerical statuses and multipliers are proposals only. A consequence combines
original rows with explicit nonnegative integer weights, divides every
coefficient exactly, and floors the new RHS. Branch bounds select proposals
but never become hidden premises of a shared cut. Integer witnesses still
pass exact original-domain/row checks and the existing execution verifier.
Infinity is never replaced by an invented finite count cap.

CountIntegerLpLearning retains a numerical basis and a sparse working kernel.
It selects complete rows near a conflicting row, or an intersecting weighted
row when no conflict is available. Every variable in a selected row remains
present, including boundary variables and fixed variables. Omitted rows make
this a relaxation; omitted columns receive no numerical suggestion. A local
numerical failure cannot prove the full model infeasible. Kernel limits are
128 columns, 512 rows and 4096 terms inside the existing sparse model limits.
Unsupported bounds, memory refusal or local work exhaustion discard a hint.

Default portfolio admission waits for the initial strategies and compilation.
One bounded scout must produce a new exact consequence before retaining an
INTEGER_LP arm. The scout is not renewed by parent handoffs. Its cost belongs
to common preparation, not the preceding strategy's feedback. Subsequent LP
calls share the owning LCG slice's remaining work. Original Boolean LP and
ordinary LCG paths retain their ordering.

A rounded integer point from that same scout now has a separate one-shot
INTEGER_LP_HINT entry, even when no cut is found. Its validation uses the
scout's remaining allowance. Unrepresented coordinates are completed at the
current lower endpoint only as a proposed assignment; every view row and
domain is checked exactly before admission. The normal original-coordinate
restoration and execution/seed validation still follow. Rejection consumes
the hint once and leaves retained exact engines available. The hint never
proves infeasibility, restarts another LCG, or runs in proof-only mode.

This admission rule matters. Broad early admission displaced all three main
portfolio orders of wide-68. Delayed, evidence-based admission preserves those
conclusions. The direct integer LP arm is useful but is not a replacement for
ordinary LCG: on 132 existing wide models at 2M work, direct integer LP solved
122 versus ordinary retained LCG's 125. Both agree with recorded truth. On
wide-68 alone, the new arm needs 41,934 work; ordinary LCG exhausts a 500k cap.
That direct boundary and all three main-portfolio orderings are maintained in
tests/regression/count-boundaries.json. This is no claim about JVM throughput
or a general improvement in the main planner's coverage.

CountRowTemplates belongs to the immutable GraphCompiler catalog. It retains
how to combine original material rows: material identity, each parent's
complete recipe-ID/coefficient map, nonnegative weights and a divisor. It does
not retain an old RHS or an old missing-material verdict. At the next request,
stock and goal changes are substituted into the current parents, the exact
combination is rebuilt, and CountProof independently verifies the derivation.
Recipe reordering is harmless; changed/added/removed producers or an omitted
external-material row fail the full-row match. There is no hash-only match.
Completed replayed rows enter the existing request-owned count cut pool.

Identity-coordinate normalized views can now retain derivations too. Each
premise must independently match the ENTIRE original material row divided by
a positive integer, including exact coefficient division and a floored RHS.
The template saves that divisor separately from the final combination/divisor.
Replay reconstructs all intermediate floors on current RHS values and emits
a multi-step CountProof derivation. Combining denominators and flooring only
at the end would weaken some consequences, so replay does not flatten them.

Fixed-variable substitution, coefficient capping, branch bounds, objective
cutoffs and production-only rows are not accepted merely because they belong
to an equivalent view. They need a matching unconditional material proof;
otherwise template admission fails. Unmapped affine derivations remain out
of scope. Reduced/affine cut sharing continues inside the existing request-owned
CountViewCuts scope. Conditional/transformed cross-request templates still
require explicit premise/mapping reconstruction.
Residency is bounded to 32 templates / 2 MiB and exposed in cache metrics.
Replay also reserves simultaneous result rows and proof workspace. Its
request-local signature cache is capped at 64 KiB; a changed supply row cannot
silently grow an unbounded validation cache. Small-budget refusal preserves
the catalog templates and never becomes a negative search conclusion.

Validation
----------

IntegerLpLearningTest independently enumerates 400 integer models and 1600
branch domains, checks exact derivations against all original witnesses, and
replays serialized proofs. It covers large offsets, fixed/negative domains,
unbounded boundaries, 2048-column outer models, cancellation, memory/work
refusal, portfolio admission and original-row proof retention.

CountRowTemplatesTest covers 120 stock/demand/recipe-order changes, new and
changed suppliers, external rows, invalid/branch-local certificates, sparse
reuse inside 1502 variables, 32 concurrent requests, actual count-stage import,
workspace refusal and 240 independently replayed current-RHS certificates.

CountNormalizedTemplatesTest adds 180 inventory/demand/order permutations and
360 independent serialized multi-step proofs, including negative RHS values,
a strict nested-floor example, full supplier changes, sparse 1502-column
models and cancellation. IntegerLpProposalTest checks no-cut witnesses through
downstream feedback, exact-engine survival after rejection, omitted boundary
rows, proof-mode admission and 240 cancellation points.

The standard suite, maintained count boundaries, fixture permutations and
equal-budget main-count comparison accompany this change. Reproduction and
measured totals are in tests/provenance/integer-lp-templates-20261011.json.
The full main-count comparison preserves all 790 previously conclusive
orders; 14 undecided and 18 unsupported conversions remain explicit limits.
These are standalone solver tests, not live Minecraft/AE validation.

The normalized-template and no-cut-witness follow-up is recorded separately in
tests/provenance/normalized-lp-reuse-20261011.json, against d5b35330. It passes
95 standard programs/batches and 732 fixture replays; all 822 main-count
orderings preserve their previous conclusions. This adds safe reuse coverage,
not additional solved cases in that corpus. Charged-work changes are mixed:
one wide-equality ordering increases from 1,423,113 to 1,504,092, while one
p0548 ordering decreases from 7,855,743 to 7,733,088. No general speedup or
live-game performance claim follows from those measurements.
