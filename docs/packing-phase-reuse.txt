Packing continuation ownership and repair service

CountPackingCore retains its exact enumeration when it samples PB conflict
repair. An inconclusive repair returns to the same search stack, compiled
projection and optional suffix index. It does not recreate their already paid
work. A scoped PB infeasibility proof can retire that enumeration; a workspace
refusal cannot. These conclusions concern the proposed face only, including
its fixed exterior, and are never original-model infeasibility proofs.

The early request-pressure handoff also captures a repair deadline at actual
entry. Its grant is the existing learning allowance, not the unused part of
the ordinary enumeration allowance. Worker transfers and repeated observations
cannot renew it. The late scout's measured work remains separate from the PB
window, but all phases consume the same fixed request/search account.

The owner can hold an enumeration, PB repair and optional scout simultaneously.
The exact stack uses the original owner workspace; PB and the scout reserve
their own workspaces. Resuming, scoped closure, cancellation and final disposal
release each object once. Scope data stay attached to the parked search, rather
than being recovered from a possibly wider live proposal.

CountPackingRepairTest checks 512 exhaustive Boolean/distance cases, scoped
proof status versus memory refusal, restoration and private-distance safety.
CountPackingScheduleTest checks actual early-entry grants under competing work
and worker movement. CountPackingScoutTest covers nested ownership, the single
completion event, cancellation and restoration of both parked frontiers.

Historical comparisons use the same 40M search budget and original recipe
order. A successful plan must pass independent primitive-prefix and final-target
checks. A native solver's proof about a fixed neighborhood cannot change a
historical SAT label into UNSAT. The complete historical corpus, direct-engine
variants, quality-cap variants and unconfirmed player reports are separate
coverage scopes.

At the unchanged 40M search cap, the complete 296-case packing campaign improves
from 140 to 141 independently verified positives. All 140 old positives retain
identical reported work and peak reservation (940,113,084 total reported work).
The recovered mknapcb6_09_bks canonical request uses 34,473,305 reported work and
96,196,965 bytes of peak reserved workspace. Its old run hit the search limit;
this is a new successful plan, not a claim of lower memory than that failure.
The other 155 canonical packing inputs remain unresolved.

All eight tested recipe orders (0,1,2,3,17,31,97,1009) of that recovery now pass
in serial and with four workers at the same fixed cap; the serial baseline
exhausts the cap in all eight. Sixteen mandatory execution requests guard them
with a 38M reported-work ceiling, including compilation. Recipe-prefix replay
remains independent of the count-search hint. Source/memory limits and finite
local closure are not weakened to obtain these results.

Final checks cover 72 distinct targeted runner entries across state, packing,
matching, runtime, continuation and execution tests. All 653 registered execution
requests pass, including the 16 new order/worker variants and existing cost
guards. Offline assembly, all 1478 baseline ABI entries, source/JAR correspondence
and Forge packaging with embedded AE dump pass at version 0.1.4. This is targeted
validation plus the complete packing and execution groups, not a full historical
corpus rerun or a live-server test.
