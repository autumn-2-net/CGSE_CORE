Compiled structural search completion (0.1.4)
============================================

Observed failure
----------------
The 24-group source/fuel graph recovered by deferred allocation repair still
exhausted its 40M search allowance under recipe permutations 17 and 1009.
Runtime traces showed that all three orders admitted the same allocation
shape, and even produced identical first-bin coefficients, targets and bounds.
The failed orders stopped before that bin's box enumeration or residual repair.

The problem crossed two scheduling levels. CountViewSearch selected a bounded
structural completion opportunity after the mandatory first engine visits.
IntegerCountBranch still left that portfolio after its initial 262144-work
compiled sample. Order-dependent whole-model scouts then consumed the optional
recovery graph's remaining allowance. The selected allocation frontier owned
state and an unspent opportunity, but did not receive enough execution time.
Its early closure was neither a contradiction nor an invalid graph model.

Scheduling change
-----------------
After the initial compiled sample, the branch now asks the portfolio whether
an already selected structural opportunity remains. If so, it continues that
same portfolio in batches of at most 262144 work. Completion, ineligibility or
the existing ceiling ends this preference and returns to the ordinary pipeline.
No new arm, solver, budget reservation or opportunity is created by this query.

This applies to grouped/vector allocations with certified capacity structure.
They already own request-local frontiers and bounded completion preferences.
An idle feedback interval within one of those opportunities no longer yields
the portfolio as wholly stalled. The ordinary parent deadline still yields,
and mandatory first visits still run.

Packing cores and LP neighborhoods keep their existing interleaved service.
A broader prototype ran their repair before useful competing LP cuts and lost
historical packing witnesses or exceeded their work guards. The complete
execution-boundary group detected this regression. Structural admission must
distinguish those cooperating searches from self-contained allocation problems;
an unspent local allowance alone does not justify moving every tool earlier.

The request cap, recovery-view cap, algorithm-local cap and completion ceiling
are unchanged. Resuming, migrating to another worker, or repeatedly inspecting
the remaining quantum cannot replenish them. The packing core retains its
existing separate handling of distinct repair phases; a query does not advance
or observe a new phase. Candidate-only tools remain ineligible in proof mode.

No row, domain, conflict certificate or count model is changed. Positive counts
still require original-domain validation, executable scheduling, primitive
prefix verification, and restoration through the compiled graph. A restricted
failure or exhausted opportunity cannot establish global infeasibility.

Regression coverage
-------------------
CountCompletionServiceTest is automatically discovered by the standard runner.
It exercises the actual compiled-to-outer-branch handoff on a planted integer
allocation, moves the next step to another worker, and independently checks the
resulting primitive execution and restored counts. It also checks mandatory
visits, nonrenewable opportunities, a seven-unit request tail, proof eligibility,
cancellation and complete release of reservations.

CountPackingServiceTest retains its original companion-service ledger checks.
CountProgressSchedulingTest additionally checks that a packing frontier cannot
claim the allocation preference and bypass its established LP cooperation.

The recovered graph orders are permanent execution boundaries. Their corpus
oracle independently checks original recipe prefixes, target delivery and
seed restoration. Full input orderings, baseline binaries, projected models,
runtime traces and all failed/intermediate experiments are retained locally.
Local evidence and player archives are not part of the repository change.

The final package passes 39 related Java programs and all 588 registered
execution-boundary requests, including the existing work guards and fixed or
expanded four-worker budgets. The 33 paired main-count variants, four count
controls and 17 older hard recipe orders are preserved. Build, 1478-entry ABI,
Forge embedded AE dump packaging and automatic test discovery checks pass.
This is targeted validation plus the complete execution-boundary group, not
a new full standard/all or live-host run.

The first paired source/fuel run moves from 22 to 23 solved out of 33 at the
same 40M search cap. The two formerly failing permutations of the previous
recovery also complete, at roughly 17.4M observed work. Existing successful
members of that batch and the 28 historical graph controls retain their
witnesses without a search-work increase. The old 22 successes use 8.5% less
total search work. Reservation peaks are not uniformly lower: one previously
solved graph uses 6016 additional bytes while requiring less search work.
These peaks measure budgeted workspace, not JVM RSS.

At this increment, the remaining ten source/fuel cases and direct allocation seed 31 are separate
open hard cases. This scheduling repair does not claim to solve those inputs,
or to replace future algorithm work. No public ABI, version, native dependency
or GTLCore adapter change is required.

The follow-up in vector-neighborhood-repair.txt adds bounded restricted bin
search for larger allocations and recovers six of those ten graph boundaries.
