Short equality supports in a retained candidate search (2026-10-20)
=================================================================

The remaining large sparse equality models spend their allowance in integer
bound propagation and conflict learning. A box can overlap a linear equation
without containing any integer support at one or both endpoints. Interval
propagation alone cannot remove those holes. Expanding the admission of the
dense affine lattice is a different, potentially expensive operation.

CountEqualitySupport pairs exact opposite rows in one immutable model view.
When a row has two through four unfixed nonzero coordinates, it shifts the
current box to nonnegative contributions, divides by the exact coefficient
GCD, and optionally complements the target. Prefix and suffix reachable-sum
bitsets identify the supported minimum and maximum of each original variable.
All arithmetic and acceptance are exact. Interior holes inform these endpoint
tests; they are not represented as a new public noninterval domain.

Admission limits are at most 512 variables, 2048 rows, 16384 terms, twelve
terms per equation, domain width fifteen, and normalized sum range 2048. Large
offsets and common coefficient factors are handled before range conversion.
Wide/unbounded domains and oversized ranges decline this optional analysis;
they are not expanded into unit variables or classified as infeasible.

Both directions of a paired equation share its last exact box and result.
Restoring a previous box after a backtrack recomputes it when necessary.
Cached positive hulls and empty boxes are reapplied only to identical bounds.
There is no cross-request cache, numerical acceptance, recipe deletion or
global proof based on an unsuccessful candidate search.

Composition and ownership
-------------------------
CountSupportedSearch owns a private retained CountLcg and its support analysis.
The existing bound-literal learning, backjumping, restarts and interval
propagation remain available. Discrete consequences use a conservative
explanation containing both pre-update bounds of every equation coordinate.
The parent retains all its existing frontiers and can keep using them.

The companion starts from the selected compiler view and its current certified
root bounds. It is considered only after 4,194,304 work in the view portfolio,
for sparse, non-allocation views with 129 through 512 variables. The later
admission preserves the cheap established trajectories. Construction and
search together have a lifetime limit of 1,048,576 work, inside the unchanged
request cap. Atomic propagation can cross a local deadline. Tiny grants and
worker handoffs cannot renew the lifetime limit or discard its owned state.

The wrapper exposes only original-coordinate candidates. Its local conflicts,
root bounds, learned clauses and infeasibility conclusion are not published
into other views. Proof-only tasks and proof-journal requests decline this
unexported explanation format. The normal original-count restoration and
primitive execution verification still decide whether a plan may be returned.
Workspace is reserved before pairing/DP construction and released on every
terminal, memory-refusal, cancellation and request-limit path.
The cache allowance includes both pre-update and supported endpoints up to
1024 bits, which can remain distinct after a backtrack releases the LCG trail.

Validation scope
----------------
CountEqualitySupportTest exhausts original integer tuples in 1536 boxes,
including signed coefficients, large offsets/factors, opposite wakeups,
backtracking, unsupported domains and cancellation. CountSupportedSearchTest
checks unique constructive solutions, side-row rejection, original scope,
one-unit continuations, worker handoffs and local/request cutoffs. The private
candidate explanation format is explicitly excluded from proof journals.

CountSupportedPipelineTest preserves a generated 257-variable input, its
independently planted witness and all original equations. The ordinary main
count pipeline must independently verify an executable plan below an 11M
observed-work guard at the unchanged 40M request cap. At the development
baseline 720a8141 this input takes 11,539,612 work; the supported completion
takes 10,279,682. Its returned candidate actually originates in the new arm.
The other five successful inputs in the eighteen-model construction campaign
retain their work; no old positive is lost. The twelve unresolved inputs in
that campaign remain explicitly unresolved.

The four canonical sparse equality hard cases are still UNKNOWN. Direct
CP-SAT 9.15.6755 comparisons also reach their separate ten-second, single-worker limit
on all four; their archived constructive witnesses still establish SAT. That
comparison does not establish solver equivalence or convert a timeout into a
negative oracle. Binary encoding, plain support propagation and conflict-directed
support enumeration trials are retained locally as separate experiments.

The complete count-model comparison covers 822 requests in three corpus orders:
all 792 prior conclusions retain identical observed work, with the same twelve
unresolved and eighteen unsupported records. The sparse family's separate eight
orders cover 256 requests: all 224 prior positives retain identical work, and
32 variants of the four hard models remain unresolved. These two campaigns
overlap and must not be added together as distinct canonical cases.

A balanced baseline/candidate/candidate/baseline headless timing sample, with
two warmups and three measured calls in each process, also checks the new
257-variable completion and two ordinary controls. For the supported completion,
median caller CPU changes from 820.31 to 750.00 ms (-8.57%), cumulative thread
allocation from 1,034,612,556 to 971,504,564 bytes (-6.10%), and peak reserved
workspace remains 40,884,658 bytes. The controls retain exact work/peak; their
CPU medians change by +2.44% and +3.45%, with overlapping sample ranges. Both
differences are 7.8125 ms, half a Windows CPU-clock quantum. Do not describe
this as a universal speedup. These are caller-thread samples on a shared host,
not whole-JVM CPU, live-game latency, RSS or a reduction in peak memory. An
earlier sample overlapped standard regression and is retained separately;
the figures above use the final built engine after that suite completed.

The complete standard suite passes all 347 test programs. These results and
the paired corpus campaigns precede a final conservative cache-reservation
increase; the arithmetic and search remain unchanged. The three maintained
support tests were repeated after that adjustment, also exercising 900-bit
offsets. The final offline build, source-jar correspondence, 1478-entry ABI
check and Forge/AE dump packaging pass. This is not a new run of all 20,927
historical records, and no live-game replay is claimed.

Local snapshots contain paired results, exact recipe/row orders, source and
binary versions, budgets and reproduction commands. This work does not change
GTLCore, the public host ABI, the 0.1.4 version or the total request budget.
