Bounded allocation exchanges in count jumps (2026-10-18)
======================================================

A wide-domain feasibility walk can reach a point at which changing either
coordinate increases weighted violation, while transferring one unit between
two coordinates improves it. Two simultaneous transfers can cross a barrier
that neither transfer crosses alone. These candidate directions were previously
kept as a local experiment because they did not solve the remaining source/fuel
cases. Lack of a new hard-case witness alone is not a reason to discard a correct
algorithm with acceptable comparison results.

CountJumpTransfers recognizes short positive equal-coefficient rows and records
unique pairs of unfixed finite coordinates. The directions preserve the chosen
row's total, but need not preserve other constraints. They are not variable
identifications, symmetry proofs, cuts or infeasibility certificates. Equal
columns or a failed neighborhood never exclude an original recipe/count.

The scout scores both legal unit directions of each pair using merged incident
rows and exact integer activity differences. If no improving single transfer
exists, up to four promising directed transfers are combined with a second
disjoint transfer. A shared row is scored once using the combined delta. Scores
only order candidates; all domains and every original row are checked again
before accepting a witness. Fixed coordinates, signed coefficients, nonzero
lower bounds and large integer offsets retain their original meaning.

Preserving the established search
---------------------------------
Simply inserting exchanges into every stalled ordinary walk changed its basin.
On 96 planted allocation models at the same local allowance, that experiment
gained three witnesses but lost nine old ones (34 solved -> 28). It is not the
default implementation. All original inputs and failed runs remain local.

The integrated version forks a bounded scout from the current point and row
weights. The parent keeps its own point, caches, random stream and search
progress. An unsuccessful scout is closed; the ordinary walk performs the same
pending weight update/perturbation it would have performed without that scout.
A successful scout contributes only an original-row-verified candidate.

There is at most one scout per retained wide-domain walk. It receives up to 16,384
local work units after the ordinary walk has spent at least sixteen times that
amount (262,144). Structural admission, initialization and arithmetic are
charged to the existing request; budgets are not increased. The child quota is
checked at step boundaries, as for the existing count jump. Its point and
compound cursor survive parent budget/worker handoffs. The small Boolean pair
search and one-shot jump trajectory stay on their existing path.

An earlier isolated scout at 32K preserved witnesses but displaced a cheap 36K
walk by almost 50%. The later admission preserves that short path. It does not
promise monotonic performance for every unseen model or finite work allowance.
Both parent and scout retain their own accounted workspace; optional memory
refusal resumes the parent and releases any prepared directions.

Regression scope
----------------
CountJumpTransfersTest compares 2,859 single/compound deltas against full
original-row evaluation, independently verifies eight single/compound witnesses,
and exercises row order, 70-bit offsets, deduplication, memory refusal, one-unit
handoffs, worker migration and cancellation. CountJumpAllocationTest preserves
all 34 baseline witnesses among 96 planted models and pins the cheap 36K path.
The other models are known feasible by their planted assignment, but lack of
a bounded local-search witness remains UNKNOWN. Both tests are automatically
discovered by the ordinary core/standard/full entry points.

At 40M search work, all 32 historical successful controls keep their outcomes
and exact work. The separate 33 source/fuel cases retain 18 successes and 15
search limits; successful cases keep their work. Four unresolved requests have
minor differences in charged total work. No new solution to those 15 limits is
claimed. At a 1,048,576 local allowance the planted comparison remains 38 solved,
with no lost baseline witness. This is capability retention with bounded
admission, not a claimed general speedup or completion of the remaining audit.

All 741 maintained fixture/order checks and the previous count/execution
regression's 17 orders preserve their results and exact work. Twelve selected
core programs and 61 count-model boundaries pass. Two archived implementation
variants fail the new allocation test as intended: replacing the walk loses a
witness, while the early isolated scout violates the short-path cost guard.
Packaging and focused-program evidence is recorded with the local case registry.
This change does not constitute a fresh complete corpus run or live
Minecraft/host execution. Version remains 0.1.2.
