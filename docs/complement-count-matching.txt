Complementary residue tables and residual-tool experiments
=========================================================
2026-10-10, baseline 2496def4.

Production change
-----------------
CountResidueJoin retains an exact sum for every quarter option mask already.
A hash slot now packs its epoch and left option mask into one long, and
reconstructs the full signed key from the two retained quarter sums. There is
no fingerprint-only equality check. The maximum left mask has 26 bits; it
cannot overlap the epoch. Every collision still charges a hash inspection.

Let S be the total of the two left quarters. Complementing every left choice
maps sum x to S-x, and residue r to (S-r) modulo the residue modulus. After
probing a COMPLETE table for r, probe this second residue with the same table:
look up S-target+rightSum, then complement the returned left option mask.
The arithmetic is covered by the parent's signed-long sum certificate; large
original lower bounds are restored and checked with BigInteger as before.

An incomplete table cannot cover its complementary residue. Truncation keeps
the old partial-table behavior and the complement must receive its own build.
Neither exhaustion nor truncation proves UNSAT. The covered-residue scan,
both probe cursors, hash-collision cursor and epoch survive local pauses.
No request limit or work weighting changes; cancellation and memory ownership
remain shared with the parent and other workers.

On subset-n48-bits48 the child reservation falls from 33,719,808 to 16,942,640
bytes. These are charged workspace reservations, not measured JVM heap/RSS.
A separate 20 MiB regression admits a maximum-capacity table and verifies a
unique witness; the former layout cannot enter that workspace.

Equal-cap results
-----------------
The complete 274-model count corpus in three orders remains 787 PASS,
17 UNKNOWN and 18 unsupported conversions at 20M: no gained/lost conclusions
or contradictions. Total recorded work is 953,777,704 versus 953,776,600.
This negligible difference is not a whole-planner performance improvement.
All 72 parity/neighbor checks retain their verdicts.

The direct retained MITM entry has a separate, explicit 80M diagnostic cap.
Use the full-corpus shuffles, not permutations generated from a selected subset:

  subset-n48-bits48     Baseline work/result       Current work/result
  Original             76,968,526 SAT             63,370,456 SAT
  Corpus order 1        12,696,421 SAT             16,535,703 SAT
  Corpus order 2        80,000,001 UNKNOWN         66,383,404 SAT

The original order uses 17.7% less work. Order 1 costs 30.2% more because the
second probe changes visitation order. Order 2 gains a verified witness.
These direct results do NOT assert main-planner success at 20M or uniform
improvement under all permutations. All three direct boundaries now run in
the regular suite with their engine and cap named explicitly. Historical
corpus expectations and existing main-count boundaries are unchanged.

CountComplementJoinTest checks independently unique subsets, signed/permuted
coefficients, partial-table coverage, the signed-long arithmetic boundary,
large original offsets, low-memory admission and identical whole/sliced results.
CountResidueJoinTest retains its 240 finite models / 720 orders / 489,600
exhaustively enumerated assignments plus cancellation/shared-budget checks.
Three deliberately broken variants are rejected: complementing a partial
table, omitting the left-mask complement, and restoring the old reservation.

The 68 existing/current standard programs pass; the final extended complement
test and all three boundary batches also pass. All 3,117 graph fixture/order
checks retain their result AND work against 2496def4. The 57 captured-stock
requests cover one worker, four workers with the same cap, and four workers
with the explicit production 2x allowance. Offline assembly and byte-for-byte
source-JAR comparison pass. These captures are not fresh server execution.
Detailed input/source hashes and results are in
tests/provenance/complement-count-matching-20261010.json.

SCIP / CP-SAT review and experiments
----------------------------------
The existing CountNeighborhood, CountFeasibilityPump and CountDiving already
cover bounded RENS, feasibility-pump and lock-guided diving ideas. Adding the
same names again would not supply a missing inference. Sources inspected:

https://github.com/scipopt/scip/blob/master/src/scip/heur_rens.c
https://github.com/scipopt/scip/blob/master/src/scip/heur_feaspump.c
https://github.com/scipopt/scip/blob/master/src/scip/heur_shiftandpropagate.c
https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/stable/ortools/sat/feasibility_jump.cc
https://github.com/google/or-tools/blob/stable/ortools/sat/cp_model_expand.cc

CP-SAT's general feasibility jump also builds and backtracks compound moves,
with discounted currently satisfied constraints. CGSE's established pair walk
is more restricted. Another distinct gap is retaining finite-domain holes
with explanations, rather than keeping only interval endpoints or a stride.
These observations motivated local experiments on all 32 sparse-equality
models, with a 20M direct-tool allowance:

  Binary coordinate expansion + PB          23 SAT / 9 UNKNOWN
  Binary coordinate expansion + LCG         19 SAT / 13 UNKNOWN
  Exact directed two-variable repair         4 SAT / 28 UNKNOWN
  Finite-domain bitset support + DFS         19 SAT / 13 UNKNOWN
  Discounted compound-move prototype         6 SAT / 26 UNKNOWN

None of these prototypes resolves any of the four remaining n144/n257 cases.
These are independent direct entries, not equal-cost main-portfolio gains.
Binary experiments check the expanded model; they were not wired into an
original-coordinate production view. Finite-support DFS lacks explanation
learning and the compound prototype is not a complete CP-SAT implementation.
No experimental solver or new portfolio arm is included in this change.
Single-equality affine search and an exact sphere-enumeration prototype also
fail to improve the 48-bit subset boundary; they remain local experiments.

Native OR-Tools 9.15.6755 and PySCIPOpt 6.2.1 were rerun on the four large
sparse equalities, one worker and 10 seconds per model. All eight attempts
return UNKNOWN/time limit, with no witness. CP-SAT also times out on the
48-bit subset. That SCIP call does not return under its soft limit; only the
owned test process was stopped after 244.5 seconds total process runtime.
The native comparison is incomplete for that last call and does not rank
solvers by speed. Native seconds are not CGSE logical work, and a timeout
does not change a planted SAT expectation into UNSAT.

What remains
------------
Five original-order cases still reach the main stage's 20M budget. Improving
finite support with explanation learning, larger sparse lattice kernels and
bounded compound search remain candidate research directions, not finished
production capabilities. A new arm needs a reproducible equal-cap gain and
must preserve the cheap graph/linear frontiers before admission. The audit's
separate host-residency and end-to-end attribution gaps remain open.
